# Campaign strategies

Select one primary strategy and one fallback for every album/campaign. Record why it fits the listener, recording, existing audience, channel access and available cash. An album is a coherent body of work; its marketing strategy does not rewrite its musical identity.

| Strategy | Best use | Sequence | Evidence to advance | Main cost/risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Single ladder | New artist or unproven album demand | Three coherent lead singles, shared world, album after learning | Repeat listening and recall across releases; delivered packet | Splitting attention; repeated delivery work |
| World album | Strong narrative/concept and owned visual identity | One lead, second entry point, full world and sequencing, listening event | Listeners can describe the world and want more than one track | Video inflation; lore overshadowing music |
| Performance catalog | Piano, chamber, acoustic or human performance | One complete performance, motif story, repeatable catalog, optional EP | Actual performance provenance; full listens and repeat use | False performance claims; incompatible artist expectations |
| Scene test | Club tracks, DJ utility or groove experiments | Functional mix, brief DJ/scene-facing preview, selected release | Permissioned scene feedback and actual repeat/selection | Vanity streams; unclear audience |
| Sync library | Cues, game/app/film music and functional instrumental catalogs | Versioned cue/stem metadata, use-specific rights, catalog preview | Real licensing inquiries/use; delivery conformity | Composition/master rights complexity; no guaranteed demand |

A DSP album, a Suno-native Album, a YouTube collection and a licensing pack are different destinations. Native Suno organization is not a distributor receipt. Reusing the same master across a single and album preserves the existing recording identity when distributor rules permit; record metadata/ISRC continuity and never manufacture identifiers.

## Required strategy record

Audience moment; album thesis; primary strategy; fallback; three considered routes and rejection reasons; channel owner; budget tier; primary behavioral metric; observation window; planned assets; delivery dependency; stop/change rule; next useful action.

Choose strategies by feasibility first, then listener evidence and economics. Public superstar metrics are research material, not a forecast for a new artist. No decorative weighted success score can substitute for a missing channel, rights, account or recording.

## Asset ladder

1. Minimum release: approved master, cover, metadata/rights, listening page, one durable upload and three short cuts from a single visual family.
2. Expanded: alternate performance/arrangement, lyric visual, behind-the-choice episode, permissioned listener participation.
3. Flagship: narrative or performance music film, paid creative variants, press assets and optional live/world experience.

Start at level 1. A film is justified by the creative thesis or an observed campaign opportunity. It is not a prerequisite for every track. A cover/visualizer can launch while a larger film is produced.

## Organic-first launch

Use the music as the opening: a specific listener scene, recognizable hook and instrument response. One durable YouTube home, one discovery channel and a permissioned direct-listener list are enough for the first test. Consumer music and creator education have distinct destinations and consent:
- Artist/label channel: listen, share, return.
- Founder/creator channel: understand the production choice, see evidence, adopt the OS.

Process content may feature the same release but should not claim commercial success from a prompt approval or a large generated catalog.

## Community and partner workflow

Research the community's topic, format, participation rules and current submission permissions. Record a concrete reason the release belongs there, the sender account, proposed message, required consent and actual submission receipt. Agents may prepare a relevant community directory and drafts. Registering accounts, joining communities or sending messages requires the corresponding explicit operator authorization; a research task does not authorize it. Do not automate unsolicited messages or fabricate engagement through other agents.

## Paid experiment workflow

Define one audience, one platform, one approved recording/creative revision, one conversion event, two creative openings and one observation window. A proposed initial test is EUR 60 total over three days (EUR 20/day aggregate), inside the campaign marketing allowance, with no automatic expansion. This is a planning example, not spending authorization.

Inspect downstream behavior: landing engagement, voluntary listening click, permissioned signup and later return. A DSP click is not a verified stream. Reconcile source platform spend separately from cash invoices and royalties. Stop if tracking is broken, mapping is wrong, rights change, spend is uncertain or fraudulent traffic appears. Scale only under a separately authorized cap after meaningful listener evidence; views alone are insufficient.

Spotify states that services guaranteeing streams or paid playlist placement violate its terms. Ordinary authorized advertising buys reach; it must not buy fabricated listening. Official policy: https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/third-party-services-that-guarantee-streams/

## Album review windows

D+2: technical availability, correct profile and delivery exceptions.
D+7: early creative/listener feedback and observed acquisition cost, clearly labeled immature.
D+28: comparable listening/retention window, total reconciled cost, next release decision.
D+90: catalog continuity, licensing/direct revenue and long-term demand where data exists.

Freeze original hypotheses. Keep failures, delays and negative findings in the postmortem. A public case study uses separately authorized evidence and states denominators, time windows and expenses.
