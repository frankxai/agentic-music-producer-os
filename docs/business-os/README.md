# Music Producer OS — Production & Business Edition

Version 0.1.0 · prepared 9 October 2026 · customer draft

**One operating method connects music, release campaigns and learning.** The artist owns the creative choices and catalog; agents prepare, inspect and coordinate the work through explicit evidence and permissions.

Start with [Customer edition](CUSTOMER-EDITION.md), [strategies](CAMPAIGN-STRATEGIES.md), [costs](TOOLS-AND-COSTS.md), [research](RESEARCH-AND-LEARNING.md) and [agent execution](AGENT-EXECUTION.md). The [Notion schema](NOTION-SCHEMA.sql) describes the six-database customer workspace. SQL is a schema specification for the Notion connector, not an executable Notion importer.

## One engine, two operating profiles

- Private artist operation: owned identity, album bibles, original takes, rights records, costs and audience observations.
- Reusable customer edition: same lifecycle, generic examples, configurable profiles, workflow instructions and budget planner. Private catalog, lyrics, contracts, contacts and credentials never enter this edition.
- Evidence-based teaching: a separately approved case study draws from the private operation. It distinguishes a prepared brief, generated take, selected recording, live release and measured outcome.

The portable Hermes/session distribution remains this repository's execution surface. The private operated Songcraft, factory and studio implementation lives in music-intelligence-os. Extend that core for shared state and hosted adapters; this edition does not introduce another server, catalog engine or lyric engine. A buyer can use the Notion/playbook layer manually without accessing that private repository. Hosted services, subscriptions and paid rendering are separate products.

## The complete lifecycle

| Stage | Work | Exit evidence | Reusable deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Artist contract | Listener moment, emotional promise, voice, sonic boundaries, identity, name review | Owned profile; explicit unresolved clearance | Artist profile and admission checklist |
| 2. Research | Comparable cohorts, music mechanisms, tool/policy checks, discovery channels | Dated source, population, inference and next test | Research card and reference-analysis prompt |
| 3. Album design | Thesis, narrator, recurring motif, palette, contrast, sequencing role | Album bible; track functions; budget and campaign choice | Album strategy worksheet |
| 4. Brief | One song reason, hook/motif, dominant groove, negative space | Exact revision; scoped creative approval | Simple, instrumental or score-first packet |
| 5. Produce | Human recording, hybrid editing or generated takes | Real source/export/settings; observed resource usage | Production run and provenance record |
| 6. Select | Listen to actual audio, hide generation context, compare relevant takes | Timestamped keep/revise/archive verdict bound to asset | Listening rubric and selection receipt |
| 7. Finish | Controlled edits, mix/master, technical delivery measurements | Versioned final export; fresh listening and QA | Master checklist and change lineage |
| 8. Rights & metadata | Composition/master/voice/sample rights, splits, artwork, profile IDs | Work-level evidence and accountable review | Release packet and unresolved blockers |
| 9. Visual production | Cover, visualizer, short hooks, optional narrative/performance film | Approved hashes/versions; text/likeness/credits QA | Visual brief and asset family |
| 10. Distribution | Deliver, inspect metadata/profile mapping, resolve exceptions, pitch | Actual distributor receipt and verified live URLs | Delivery runbook and reconciliation |
| 11. Amplify | Owned launch, discovery tests, permissioned communities, optional paid ads | Approved placements; spend receipts; tracking | Prioritized campaign playbook |
| 12. Observe & improve | Windowed metrics, costs, listener feedback, deviations | Source exports; conclusion scope; decision | Postmortem, knowledge card and template patch |

Work can proceed in parallel after dependencies are met: research and private music drafting while name review runs; visual briefs while the recording is finished; launch copy while delivery is pending. Spend-intensive visuals begin after audio selection. Public artist claims and release execution wait for their own evidence.

## Three equal production paths

**Human:** authored score/lyrics, recorded instruments/voice, dry originals, session files, edits and measured export. Human authorship and actual performance are recorded separately.

**Hybrid:** owned recorded/score material plus AI arrangement, lyric assistance, stems, edits or generative interpretation. Keep the original and generated derivatives, permissions, parent revisions and meaningful human decisions.

**Generated:** compact original brief into Suno or another checked provider. Save exact prompt, actual lyrics, visible model/settings, creation/download dates, plan/terms evidence and authorized export. A commercial-use permission does not itself establish copyright, performer eligibility or exclusive rights.

Current simple mode: one compact paragraph with essential musical/vocal character and two or three original hook lines; the provider writes the remainder. Instrumental mode has no vocal lines. Full lyrics and Custom Mode are explicit choices. The existing portable session CLI's advanced validation does not become a simple-mode validator because a guide says so; use the implemented studio simple contract or a truthful draft pending adapter work.

## Velocity contract

Run short complete cycles rather than accumulating unreviewed generations:
- Four proposed creation/listening tranches per week, each with one useful musical question and the currently authorized generation allowance.
- Two active campaign preparations and two marketing experiments maximum in this music lane; this is a proposed lane limit, not a portfolio-wide override.
- One selected recording produces a cover, one durable audiovisual asset, three reusable hook cuts, one release page and one process lesson.
- Weekly review prioritizes the current bottleneck: missing export, unheard take, unclear rights, wrong mapping, weak creative or unresolved spend.
- A default 28-day internal delivery target leaves room for exceptions. Existing proven catalogs may test a 14-day internal target when delivery/mapping and the platform pitch window allow it. No automatic biweekly release promise.
- Releases are readiness-driven; production and documentation can advance every week. Scheduled jobs require an actual runner and receipt.

Measure time from brief to audition, audition to frozen packet, packet to verified delivery, cost per accepted usable recording, founder minutes per campaign and repeat listening. Generation count is throughput telemetry, never the success metric.

## First seven working days

Day 1: configure artist/profile, choose one album thesis, inspect tool/account capabilities, open one campaign with a cash allowance.
Days 2–3: run the first bounded tranche, ingest authorized exports and listen; choose one controlled revision.
Day 4: bind a selected recording and its provenance; prepare a cover and three hook concepts.
Day 5: stage one truthful release packet and an organic-first marketing plan.
Day 6: package one sanitized process walkthrough and fix the workflow friction it exposes.
Day 7: review costs, blockers and observations; produce a versioned template improvement.

These are a proposed work sequence, not booked dates, paid authorizations, submitted releases or a claim that all work finishes within seven days.
