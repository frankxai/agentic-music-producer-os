# Music fundamentals

Revision 2026-10-01. These are conditional craft methods, not a formula for a hit.
Use only the modules relevant to the work. Project canon and the current artist
contract outrank defaults. No artist identity, private taste or account data is
part of this public guide.

## Premise and listener

Choose the listener's situation, the speaker, the person addressed, the pressure,
and the change. Find a sentence someone could say in that scene. For a short club
hook, a bodily/social action can carry the whole idea; do not force a three-act
story into four lines. For an instrumental, name what the motif does over time.
Define the result: a dancefloor loop, a performed song, a score, a narration bed,
a game loop, or a sync cue. Each has different success criteria.

## Harmony and voice leading

Choose tonal center, mode, harmonic rhythm, and a tension/release plan when useful.
Do not equate minor with sadness or major with happiness. Track common tones and
small voice movements; let bass inversions change the weight of a repeated chord.
A pedal can make an upper change feel large without moving the foundation. Use
borrowed chords, suspensions, extensions or modulation for a section's purpose.
For exact harmony, compose MIDI/MusicXML and perform it; a text prompt is a request.

## Rhythm, meter and groove

BPM does not define feel. Specify meter, subdivision, straight/swung feel, backbeat,
kick placement, syncopation and note lengths. Separate a half-time sensation from
half-speed playback. Leave holes for vocal consonants and instrumental answers.
Use a stable pulse with selective displacement when it supports the genre. Meter
changes and polyrhythm need a clear anchor. Confirm timing by ear and on a grid;
do not infer exact BPM merely from the requested value.

## Melody and motif

Start with a small recognizable cell: contour, rhythm, interval shape and landing
tone. Vary one dimension at a time: sequence, augmentation, inversion, displacement,
register, or instrumental transfer. Save the high note for a meaningful word or
structural peak. Contrast phrase lengths, pickup/strong-beat entries, and rests.
Write within a performer's comfortable tessitura, not just their extreme range.
A repeated note can carry pressure; a leap can open the answer. Test the sung result.

## Lyrics, point of view and subtext

Decide who knows what and when. Replace claims of feeling with action, detail, or
withheld speech where that makes the line stronger. Keep an image world coherent,
but allow a deliberate disruption at a turn. Verse two must change the information
or stakes. A bridge can reveal a cost, change the addressee, or strip away an excuse.
Do not force a bridge or final key change into every song. Short hook forms can be
complete without verses. Original reference analysis retains mechanisms, not phrases.

## Hooks, titles and repetition

Compare meaning, mouthfeel, musical behavior, memory and voice separately. Try a
spoken sentence before ornamenting it. Design returns: AAAB chant, ABAB exchange,
AABA with a pressure line, call/response, or progressive mutation. Repetition must
have a job: establish, insist, invite, implicate, fracture or pay off. Exact title
hits are counts, not an artistic score. There is no universal repetition quota.
Do not publish a spray of drafts when one selected direction serves the request.

## Prosody, rhyme and pronunciation

Mark lexical stress and compare it with musical stress. Count syllables as a guide;
pronunciation, elision and melisma can change the sung count. Dictionary ambiguity
and non-English text require a pronunciation source or explicit uncertainty. Put
vowels that the singer can sustain on long notes; shorten consonant clusters at
speed. Rhyme can reinforce or undercut meaning. Use internal echo, slant rhyme,
assonance and consonance without twisting syntax. Speak the lyric at pulse, then
sing it; a text scan cannot prove a topline fits a melody.

## Form, attention and arrangement

Give each section a job. Track the foreground object, supporting objects, register,
density, rhythm, space and harmonic pressure. Create contrast by subtraction,
silence, an unanswered phrase, a new bass rhythm, or a change in vocal distance.
The last chorus can mean something new without adding instruments. An arrangement
map should say what changes and what remains recognizable. Stems do not recover
perfect original sources; inspect separation artifacts before mixing.

## Vocal direction and casting

Specify register/tessitura, grain, onset, articulation, breath, timing, phrase arc,
distance and harmony role. Keep singer identity in the project-owned casting/canon
file. Observe the account's authorized Voice/Persona at execution, with consent
evidence. A generic voice description is not a clone authorization. Preserve
unusual words only when the speaker and delivery can carry them.

## Drums, bass and low end

Assign kick/sub/body/texture roles. Decide who owns the attack and who sustains the
low note. Control note length, glide, sidechain envelope and distortion in a DAW
after listening. Center low fundamentals when the delivery context needs stable
mono translation; wide upper harmonics can remain musical. Check phase, masking,
small-speaker audibility and kick/bass interaction. A bass adjective proves none
of these measurements.

## Space, dynamics and mixing

Distinguish width from depth, and dry foreground from wet support. Use pre-delay,
decay, early reflections and automation to move attention. Keep a clear focal
source before adding layers. Balance first, then correct specific masking or
resonance; loudness is not clarity. Use reference listening at matched loudness
and in several playback contexts. Preserve source audio and export a new version.

## Master and delivery

Select a destination profile rather than enforcing one LUFS value everywhere.
Measure integrated LUFS, true peak, loudness range, duration, channels, sample rate
and codec. A two-pass normalization must be remeasured on the written file and
checked against the original checksum. Silence/corruption hold the handoff.
Normalization is delivery engineering; it does not certify musical quality,
copyright, exact notes, perceptual mastering or lossless source quality.

## Production decisions and feedback

Separate text review, rendered listening, technical measurement and rights
attestation. For every take, record provider/model, exact packet hash, account
entitlement receipt, job/take ID, source and archive checksums, observations with
timestamps, decision and next single change. Keep failures. A three-success
heuristic is not scientific proof; test across contexts and counterexamples before
promoting a pattern. Personal preferences remain owner scoped.

## Rights, provenance and release

Track lyric/composition authorship, source recordings, samples, voice consent,
provider terms/account tier at generation and download, and intended distribution.
Do not infer rights from a subscription name or an online source. Archive dated
receipts and check the destination's current policies. Unknown rights hold release;
they need not prevent an original text draft. Publishing and paid operations need
their own authorization. No catalog upload, revenue claim or release status is
implied by generating a file.

## Genre and use-case decisions

| Work | Priority | Useful contrast | Verify on audio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Club hook | Short cells, physical/social action, pocket | Dry call / instrumental answer | Loop seam, kick/sub, chant mouthfeel |
| Pop/indie song | Distinct speaker, memorable turn | Verse detail / chorus thesis | Topline, stress, section payoff |
| Rap/hip-hop | Cadence, internal rhyme, breath and perspective | Dense flow / negative space | Syllable attack, breath, groove |
| R&B/soul | Vocal phrasing, harmony and restraint | Held note / conversational entry | Intonation, melisma, lyric intelligibility |
| Rock/metal | Riff identity, attack and dynamic shape | Register/density or half-time shift | Guitar/bass separation, articulation |
| Jazz/acoustic | Interaction, voice leading and performance | Space / response, reharmonized return | Human phrasing, harmonic coherence |
| Score/cinematic | Motif, orchestration and narrative cue | Timbre/register transfer | Actual notes, transition, timing to picture |
| Ambient/narration bed | Unobtrusive evolution and speech space | Texture / silence | No surprise peaks or competing vocal |
| Game/brand cue | Identity, edit points and repeatability | One memorable cell / clean resolve | Seam, target length, edit handles |

Genre ranges and instrument counts are starting choices, never compulsory rules.
Wellness/frequency language is an artistic frame; it does not prove a medical effect
or an exact tuning/binaural signal. Synthesize and measure precise tones separately.
