# Genre and Mood Lexicon

Attribute vocabulary for writing style prompts without naming artists, bands, or tracks.

Load this when a brief names a vague genre ("something jazzy", "epic", "lo-fi") and you need
to convert it into pocket, harmony, timbre, vocal, and production decisions.

---

## How to use

Every genre entry below is a **default, not a rule**. Take three or four attributes, discard
the rest, and let the discarded space become the arrangement's negative space. A style prompt
built from every line of an entry will sound like stock library music.

The order that matters: **pocket → harmonic color → focal timbre → vocal persona → production era.**

## Genre attribute sets

### Hip-hop family

| Sub-lane | Pocket | Harmony | Focal timbre | Vocal | Production |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boom-bap | 82–96 BPM, swung 16ths, snare behind the grid | Minor 7ths, sampled loops, static harmony | Dusty piano/horn loop, vinyl noise floor, upright-ish bass | Conversational, mid-register, pocket-locked | Narrow stereo, filtered highs, glued compression |
| Trap | 130–150 BPM felt half-time | Minor triads, sparse, ostinato | 808 slide bass, rolled hats, glassy bell or plucked lead | Ad-lib layered, autotuned or dry-declaimed | Sub-forward, wide reverb tails, empty mids |
| Jazz-hop | 76–92 BPM, brushed or loose | Extended chords, ii-V motion, real modulation | Rhodes, brushed kit, walking upright bass | Restrained, behind the beat | Warm, room-present, minimal limiting |

|---|---|---|---|---|

## Mood → musical translation

| Mood | BPM window | Modal color | Dynamic behavior | Space |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Contemplative | 60–80 | Dorian, natural minor | Gentle swells, no peak | Wide, reverberant |
| Determined | 118–140 | Mixolydian, major | Stepped build, held plateau | Present, forward |
| Longing | 68–92 | Minor with major-IV lift | Rise that never fully resolves | Medium, slightly distant |
| Euphoric | 124–132 | Major, Lydian brightness | Long build, full release | Very wide, tall |
| Menacing | 70–90 felt, 140 counted | Phrygian, semitone tension | Flat until sudden drop | Narrow, close, dry |
| Restorative | <60 or free | Drone, fifths, no cadence | Almost no variation | Oceanic, enveloping |
| Focused | 120–128 constant | Modal, minimal harmony | Deliberately static | Clear, organized, dry |

## Emotional gradient ladders

Pick a rung, not a bucket. Precision here beats adjective stacking.

```text
Energy:     tranquil → peaceful → calm → contemplative → steady → moving → driving → intense
Brightness: subterranean → dark → shadowed → warm → open → radiant → luminous
Density:    bare → minimal → clean → balanced → textured → layered → dense
Intimacy:   whispered → close → conversational → present → projected → belted → roared
Cleanliness: pristine → polished → natural → worn → saturated → degraded → ruined
```

Two rungs from two different ladders usually beat five adjectives from one.

## Fusion grammar

```text
[Host genre's rhythm section] + [Guest genre's harmony or timbre] + [one deliberate anachronism]
```

The rhythm section decides which genre the listener thinks they are hearing. The guest genre
should arrive through harmony or a single focal instrument, not through a second drum kit.

Working combinations:

- Appalachian modal melody over minimal-techno pulse
- Bossa pocket with sub-bass and glassy digital pads
- Gospel voice-leading over a dry, unglamorous drum machine
- Chamber strings inside a boom-bap noise floor
- Free-time ambient drone interrupted by a single strict-tempo section

Failed combinations are almost always **two competing rhythmic identities**, not clashing timbres.

## Negative-space prompts

Naming what is absent shapes arrangements more reliably than adding adjectives:

```text
no cymbals until the final chorus
no bass below the bridge
one instrument at a time, never two sustained together
percussion enters only after the first hook lands
the low end stays empty so the voice owns the bottom
```

## What not to write

- Named artists, bands, songs, labels, franchises, or "sounds like" comparisons.
- "Professional", "high quality", "best", "amazing" — these describe nothing actionable.
- More than six named instruments.
- A BPM number with no pocket description; 90 BPM straight and 90 BPM swung are different songs.
- Contradictory pairs: euphoric + sparse, aggressive + restorative, dense + spacious.
